# Channel Discovery

Compare acquisition channels. For a list of specific groups with direct URLs, use [community-discovery](../community-discovery/SKILL.md).

1. Establish the audience, buying process, product and stage, budget, timeline, available effort, and prior channel results. Use available context and ask only about missing inputs that could change the ranking.
2. Apply the [channel research guide](references/channel-research.md) when mapping channels or validating candidate costs, reach, or competitor activity. Assess fit across paid, organic, and outbound channels. Investigate the three to five strongest candidates and verify current costs and timelines for the target market.
3. Before recommending paid acquisition, check [funnel readiness](references/funnel-readiness.md), including retention, activation, conversion, and acquisition economics.
4. Score each viable channel from 1 to 5 on audience fit, speed, cost efficiency, effort, and learning value. Rank by total, breaking ties by speed, learning value, then cost efficiency. For early-stage teams, prioritize channels that can prove or disprove the hypothesis quickly.
5. Report the audience and constraints, a compact score matrix, and up to three recommendations. Prioritize one or two channels and use the third as a fallback. For each, give evidence of fit, a first experiment, a timeline, cost or effort, and a success criterion. Note deferred channels when the conditions for reconsidering them matter.
